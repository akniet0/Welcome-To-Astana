History of the city

The history of Astana begins in 1830 with the construction of the Akmola fortress on the Ishim River. A city grew around it, becoming an important trade and economic center of the region.

In 1879, a railway connecting Tyumen and Akmolinsk was proposed. On July 6, 1994, the decision was made to move the capital from Almaty to Akmola, and the official transfer took place on December 10, 1997.

On May 6, 1998, Akmola was renamed Astana, and in 1999 it was awarded the title of a "city of peace" by UNESCO. Since 2000, the city has been a member of the International Assembly of Capitals and Large Cities.

Nomadic Roots & Traditions

Hospitality (Qonaqjaylyq): Welcoming every guest with an open heart, fresh tea, and warm shelter is one of the most sacred principles of nomadic culture.

The Sacred Dombra: The two-stringed wooden lute holds the soul of Kazakh oral literature, narrating historic legends, battles, and poetic kyis across generations.

The Yurt (Kiiz Üy): A mobile felt sanctuary harmonizing family life with the natural seasons of the steppe, symbolizing unity, peace, and balance with the cosmos.

Nauryz Meiramy: The ancient celebration of spring renewal and new beginnings, bringing communities together for games, performances, and communal feasts.

Traditional Flavors to Try

Beshbarmak: The iconic centerpiece of Kazakh feasts, featuring tender boiled cuts served over broad hand-rolled noodles and drenched in savory onion broth.

Golden Baursaks: Puffy, golden spheres of dough fried to a tender crisp, served steaming hot at every festive table and family gathering.

Steppe Qurt & Dairy: Sun-dried sour curd balls and refreshing dairy drinks like qymyz and shubat that carried nomads across journeys across the vast steppe.
